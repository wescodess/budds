1Biology notes: Enzymes and temperature

Let’s rebuild the missing part

Every enzyme has a temperature range where its shape holds and its active site fits the substrate. Inside that range, warming speeds the reaction because molecules collide more often. Past the top of the range, the bonds holding the shape break, the active site deforms, and activity falls fast.1

Where that range sits depends on where the organism lives. Enzymes from hot springs hold their shape far above body temperature; enzymes from cold-water fish start to unfold at temperatures we would call mild.1
